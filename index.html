Product vision & positioning

Durbar is Ghana's smart event command center. It combines event pages, invitations, RSVP, guest management, MoMo payment & contribution tracking, vendor coordination, an AI planning assistant, reminders, ticketing, check-in and post-event memories — designed for how Ghanaians actually run events: on WhatsApp, with mobile money, by families and committees.

Positioning line: “Durbar — plan it, invite them, collect the money, run the day. The all-in-one event command center for Ghana.”

Working brand name is Durbar (a grand Ghanaian gathering). Alternatives if you prefer: Afahye, Boafo, Fie, Nhyira. Easy to swap — say the word.

The honest principle

We will not build a bloated everything-app on day one. Durbar wins one category first — Ghana weddings, traditional ceremonies, birthdays and private parties — then earns the right to expand into public/ticketed events, sports and community groups. Everything on this site is organized around that discipline: a focused MVP, then a scalable roadmap.

Deliverable · Market research & the unsolved problem

The gap nobody in Ghana has closed

We searched the Ghanaian event landscape — ticketing platforms, funeral tools, wedding checklists, vendor pages and the global incumbents. Here is the one painful, recurring complaint that no major platform serving Ghana has solved.

⭐ THE CRITICAL UNSOLVED PAIN POINT

Nobody can reconcile the money and the people for a committee-planned event.

Ghanaian events are funded and organised collectively — wedding committees, family funeral committees, church and old-students groups. Money flows in as pledges → partial payments → cash → MoMo from dozens of different phone numbers and the diaspora. Money flows out to many vendors, paid by different people. There is no single trusted, shared ledger that answers, in real time and visible to the whole committee:

  • Who pledged, and who has actually paid?
  • How much is still out there to collect?
  • What have we spent, what do we still owe vendors, and what's the real balance?

Today this lives in one overwhelmed person's head, a paper notebook, and three WhatsApp groups — so, as families told researchers, “the total spend only becomes clear after the funeral,” often after the family has gone into debt.

What the evidence shows

✅ Already solved — public ticketing

eGotickets, Tix.africa and Ayatickets already sell tickets to strangers with MTN MoMo, USSD and WhatsApp. Eventbrite struggles in Ghana only because it has no mobile-money support and forces foreign cards. So selling concert tickets is a crowded, solved space — not our wedge.

⚠️ Half-solved — money in

Funeral & memorial tools (donation links, FuneralDon, M-Changa) collect money one-way via a link. They do not reconcile against a named contributor list, pledges, or expenses, and they're disconnected from the guest list, RSVP and vendors.

❌ Unsolved — committee pledges

Wedding & funeral committees run on pledges that members routinely fail to fulfil. Tracking happens in WhatsApp groups and ad-hoc spreadsheets. There is no tool that turns a pledge into a tracked, reminded, reconciled payment.

❌ Unsolved — headcount vs the pot of food

With everything on WhatsApp, no one has a real headcount, so food runs out or money is wasted. Nothing connects "480 people said yes" to "you budgeted 350 plates."

❌ Unsolved — vendor trust

Deposits are paid with no contract and no recourse; vendors go silent or fail to show. No verification, reviews, receipts or escrow exists for everyday Ghanaian events.

❌ Unsolved — it's all scattered

Planning is fragmented across WhatsApp, spreadsheets, phone calls, MoMo messages and vendor DMs. No command center pulls it together for the people actually running the event.

Why the global apps can't just copy this

Eventbrite, WithJoy, Evite and Luma are built for a Western model: one host, card payments, a guest who clicks "yes." None of them have a committee contribution & pledge-reconciliation ledger, because Western events aren't funded by 40 relatives sending MoMo from 12 networks. This is a structurally Ghanaian (and West-African) problem — which is exactly why a Ghana-native product can own it.

Our wedge → our moat

Wedge: be the Contribution & Committee Ledger for weddings, funerals and family events — the one trusted place where pledges, MoMo, cash, diaspora money and vendor balances reconcile in real time, visible to the whole committee. Moat: once a family's money and guest list live in Durbar, the event page, RSVP, vendors, reminders and check-in come along for free — and the next family event starts here too.

A practical planning assistant — not a gimmicky chatbot

The AI does the boring, high-value work a stressed host or committee secretary needs done. It's grounded in the event's real data (date, guest count, budget, pledges, vendor balances) so its advice is specific, not generic.

  • Generates timelines, realistic budgets and event-specific checklists.
  • Drafts invitations & WhatsApp reminders in a warm, Ghana-appropriate tone.
  • Suggests the right questions to ask each vendor type.
  • Detects risk: too many guests for the food budget, unpaid vendor balances, unfulfilled pledges, missing venue details, late reminders.
  • Summarises event status for the host in one tap.

Suggested technology stack

Chosen for low-data performance, fast iteration, a small team, and Ghana realities (weak networks, MoMo, WhatsApp). Pragmatic over trendy.

Frontend

Next.js (React) as a PWA. Server-rendered, fast event pages that work on 3G; installable without an app store. Tailwind for speed. Offline-friendly check-in via service workers + IndexedDB.

Backend

Node (NestJS) or Django. REST + webhooks. Background jobs (BullMQ/Celery) for reminders & payment reconciliation. Postgres as source of truth.

Database & infra

PostgreSQL (Supabase/Neon to start), Redis for queues/cache, object storage (S3/Cloudflare R2) for media. Cloudflare CDN — critical for low-data speed across Ghana.

Payments

Paystack and/or Hubtel for MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash, AT Money, cards & bank. USSD fallback for low-end phones. Start with manual marking, add live collection in Phase 2.

Messaging

WhatsApp Business / Cloud API (via Twilio/Meta) for invites, reminders, receipts; local SMS aggregator (Hubtel/Mnotify) fallback; email via Resend/SES.

AI

LLM via API (Claude) for drafting & planning, plus deterministic rules for risk detection (headcount vs budget, balances, deadlines). Keep AI grounded in event data; never invent numbers.

This very prototype is intentionally a single, dependency-free static site — proof that the guest surface can be tiny and instant. Production keeps that low-data spirit.

Payment & communication integration plan

💸 Payments — phased

Phase 1 (MVP): manual marking. Host/finance admin records each MoMo/cash payment; ledger reconciles; receipts & reminders generated. Zero integration risk, instantly useful.

Phase 2: live collection via Paystack/Hubtel — MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash, AT Money, cards, bank transfer. Auto-match incoming payments to contributors via reference codes. Payout to host wallet/bank.

Phase 3: USSD contribution flow (dial-a-code) for guests with no data; split-payment links; vendor deposit collection; (later) escrow.

💬 Communication — phased

Phase 1: share-to-WhatsApp links + pre-filled messages; click-to-send SMS/email; copyable invite text. No API cost to start.

Phase 2: WhatsApp Business API templates for invites, reminders, receipts & status; bulk SMS via local aggregator; delivery tracking.

Phase 3: two-way RSVP over WhatsApp (reply "YES 2"); automated reminder schedules; segmented blasts by guest group.

Monetisation hooks live here: SMS/WhatsApp bundles, payment-processing fees, vendor lead fees.

Privacy & security checklist

Designed with Ghana's Data Protection Act (2012) expectations in mind. Privacy is a feature, because we hold phone numbers, money and photos.

  • Guest lists private by default — never public unless host opts in
  • Role-based access (owner/admin/finance/guest-manager/check-in/viewer)
  • Consent capture for phone numbers, photos & contact import
  • Self-service data deletion & export for hosts and guests
  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest for PII & payment refs
  • OTP / phone-based auth; optional 2FA for finance roles
  • Full audit log on every money entry (who recorded what, when)
  • No card/MoMo PINs stored — tokenised via the payment provider
  • Minimise data: collect only what the event needs
  • Clear cancellation/refund rules & receipts; dispute trail
  • Photo galleries respect consent; takedown on request
  • Vendor verification & anti-fraud checks on the roadmap

Vendor marketplace roadmap

Stage 0 — MVP: private vendor tracking

Hosts add their own vendors (contact, quote, deposit, balance, due date, files). No marketplace yet — but we're quietly building the category/city/price dataset.

Stage 1 — Directory

Browse vendors by city & category (photographer, caterer, decorator, MC, DJ, makeup, venue, security, rentals, transport). Inquiry forms. We charge lead fees / featured listings.

Stage 2 — Profiles & trust

Portfolios, packages, availability, verification badges, ratings & reviews tied to real bookings. This directly attacks the vendor-trust pain point.

Stage 3 — In-app booking & payments

Quote → book → deposit via MoMo → balance reminders → receipt — all in Durbar. Take a small booking/processing fee.

Stage 4 — Escrow & guarantees

Hold deposits, release on milestones, dispute resolution. The strongest anti-fraud trust layer — and a serious moat. (Regulatory work required.)

Risks & how we reduce them

⚠️ "Just use WhatsApp" inertia

Reduce: be WhatsApp-native, not a replacement. The only thing we ask people to leave WhatsApp for is the money truth they can't get there.

⚠️ Payment trust & fraud

Reduce: audit log on every entry, receipts, role-based finance access, manual-first then licensed aggregators, escrow later.

⚠️ Low-data / weak networks

Reduce: tiny pages, offline check-in, USSD/SMS fallbacks, CDN. Performance is a feature.

⚠️ Seasonality & one-off use

Reduce: families have many events; capture the committee & guest data so the next event starts here. Push recurring groups/dues for steady usage.

⚠️ Overbuilding

Reduce: the postpone list is enforced. Win weddings/funerals before anything else.

⚠️ Monetising too early

Reduce: free basic pages; charge where value is obvious (SMS bundles, fees, vendor leads, planner/org plans), never tax ordinary hosts upfront.

⚠️ Privacy mishandling

Reduce: consent, deletion, minimisation, RBAC, Data Protection Act alignment from day one.

⚠️ Incumbents add MoMo

Reduce: our moat isn't payments — it's the committee ledger + Ghana event graph + vendor trust, which they can't quickly replicate.
